# A maths question to work out, on the canvas

summary: Help with homework or a maths question (typed, or from a photo): a sum, adding or taking away, a fraction of a number, division, times tables, my 7 year old, a question number, which one first, pen on iPad, hints and pictures.
reason: only if the person wants help with a maths or homework question (typed, or photographed alone or with others), and did not ask for a document

A maths question the child answers herself, typed or in a photo: draw it on the canvas. The question as given, and the method laid out whole: a picture of one part (one vase with its flowers, the others beside it) and numbered steps, each saying what to do with its result left blank, never the answer. One `ask` for the final answer (`is=` every form you accept) that she writes with the pen or types, and a hint behind a tap. When the parent asks for the method, the working or a picture, that is the whole answer, not the question again. A photo of several questions with none named: only `say title | Which one first?` and a `say choice | <number>. <question>` each; a tap on one gives that question. A number named ("question 15"): go straight to it.

**Use it when** a child, or a parent for a child, needs help with a maths question, typed or from a photo, even asked as visual, step by step or with the pen.

**Not when** an adult wants it explained, or a document or worksheet is asked for.

**Example**
```series
scene stack
say title | Flowers in vases
say | 5 vases. Each has 6 yellow, 2 blue and 4 green flowers.
say | Step 1: how many flowers in one vase? Add the three colours.
say | Step 2: that many in each of the 5 vases. How many altogether?
ask a1 is=60,sixty | Flowers altogether: __
hint | Count one vase first.
hint | Now 5 vases of that many.
```

**What failed** The question restated with a blank and nothing else when the method was asked for; the answer in a label or a step; every in-between value written on the picture.
