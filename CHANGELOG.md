# What's new

Newest first. Each item is something the brain can show live when asked "showcase what's new": draw it, don't describe it. The server reads this file.

## Read aloud (new)
- There is a speaker button in the message box, beside the microphone. Turn it on and Conduit reads its answers out loud as they appear: the words, the headings, the questions it asks you, and a hint when you tap it. Choices are read at the end ("You can choose: A, B or C"). Labels on a drawing, code, tables and chart numbers are never read. Tap any block of words on the screen to hear just that one. While it is speaking the button becomes a stop button.
- It uses the voice that is already on your iPad or Mac, so nothing is sent anywhere. In the + menu, "Voice and speed" picks the voice and slow, normal or fast (slow to start with). It stops when you send a message, press Stop, record a voice note, or leave the page. Maths like "1 000 = ___ ÷ 3" is read as "1000 equals blank divided by 3".

## Voice and drawing, out of the way (live)
- The drawing tools fold down to one small pen in the corner, so they never sit on the answer. The pen still draws while they are folded, and tapping it opens them again. Your choice is remembered on that device; a phone starts folded.
- Recording happens inside the message box now, no box pops up. While you talk it shows the sound and the time, with a cross to throw it away, a tick to turn it into words you can edit, and an arrow to send it straight away.
- If the mic hears nothing for three seconds it stops and tells you, instead of letting you talk into nothing. A recording is only ever sent once.
- The microphone is asked for once and kept for a couple of minutes, so the iPad stops asking each time.

## Words inside a card stay in the card (live)
- Words written in a card now stand in that card, in the order they were written, above or under its choices; a line that answers a choice sits right under it. Before, they were all drawn at the top left of the screen, on top of each other and on the title. Words that belong to a thing and were not told where to stand now go one under another, so text never lands on other text.

## Report a problem (live)
- Report a problem, in the + menu, saves what is on screen and what just happened, for fixing.

## A character that runs off runs off whole (live)
- A character you drew in parts, sent off to the right of the picture, now goes as one: nothing stretches into a long band across the screen, in a quick move or a slow one, and it stays itself when it rests at the edge. Sent past the edge, it leaves the picture.

## Every starter shows on a phone (live)
- On a phone the first screen now shows all seven starters. Before, the first one could go missing and leave a gap, on some phone sizes. If the choices do not fit, they are set a little smaller and closer together instead of being dropped.

## Maths help happens on the canvas (live)
- Ask for help with a maths question, typed or photographed, and the whole screen becomes that one question: large and centred, with a blank to write the answer in with the pen. Nothing else is on screen, no paragraphs and no earlier answers.
- The model now only says the question and its answer, in one `problem` line. The page draws everything else the same way every time, so it no longer depends on what the model happens to draw. A question it cannot read as a sum (a word problem) is a plain workspace: the question, the blank, the pen and Check.
- Check, Hint and Type it sit just under the blank. Check reads the handwriting (the small reader the lesson uses; a server on the Codex brain refuses it, and the typed field is the fallback) and marks it on the page, with no model call. Typed "3 000", "3,000" and "3000" all count.
- Hints come from the question's own numbers: "What do we know?" lights the numbers and the sign, "What are we finding?" lights the blank, "Try a picture" opens a picture. They never show the answer. After a wrong try, "Show me" plays a picture through and stops before the answer.
- Small chips under the question draw a layout from its numbers, one step at a time ("1 of 4"), with labels on their parts and the unknown always a "?": equal groups and a bar for division and times, an array for times, tens and ones and a number line with jumps for adding and taking away, and a bar or groups for a fraction of a number. Big numbers (1 000) are labelled blocks, not a thousand dots. Your ink stays while a picture changes.
- Tell the brain an answer by voice or in the chat and it can put it in the blank for real (`fill p1 | 12`): it shows in the box and is checked like a typed answer. It can fill nothing any other way, and the chapter says so.
- A run of three or more underscores in plain words is no longer drawn as a blank that cannot be answered: it is shown as a short line.
- Only a maths question takes the screen: an ordinary answer that holds a quiz question stays an ordinary answer. The message box stays where it is, so you can always type. Done puts the screen back, the pen is on by default, and a finger or the pen itself presses the buttons. A photo of a whole page with no question named still gives "Which one first?", and a tap on a question gives that question's workspace.
- The pen no longer drops a stroke while a lesson saves or redraws, or after the pen leaves the screen mid-stroke.

## A wireframe you ask for comes out as a wireframe (live)
- "Wireframe a music app clone" or "mock up a banking app" now gives a mock-up of screens you can tap through, not a live app that overflows. A live app is for something that has to run.
- A live app laid out like a desktop product (a sidebar and a main view) is set in its own type, so its text stays inside its boxes.

## Works with ChatGPT too (not live)
- The app can now think with your ChatGPT subscription through the Codex command line, as well as with Claude. Nothing changes here: this server stays on Claude.
- On ChatGPT it draws, answers, looks at its own drawing and fixes it, searches the web, reads your photos and uses the canvas tools. The model picker, skills, agent mode, the critic and filling parts ahead stay with Claude. Answers arrive a message at a time rather than word by word.

## Step by step on the canvas (live)
- Ask for instructions, a recipe, a build or a how-to step by step, or give it a photo of them, and the answer is a picture with Back and Next: each step adds its part. A document of steps to work through comes only when you ask for a checklist or a document, or for a learner's worksheet.
- When you ask for a drawing while a document is open ("Build it on canvas", "Interactive step by step"), the drawing is what you see. The document is put away, one tap on "Show the document" brings it back.
- The small picture under the field after you attach a photo is the photo you attached, even after the server was restarted.

## Dashboards, questions on the stage, readable mock-ups (live)
- Ask for a dashboard or a report and get one: headline numbers with their changes, panels and lists, fetched where it can and marked approximate where it can't. On a phone the headline cards sit two to a row.
- A question can be asked right on the stage: a blank inside the sentence or a few choices, Check when you press it, hints behind a tap, "Show me" after a wrong try, and the brain hears how you got on.
- Mock-ups never set their smallest text below a readable size, every text keeps strong contrast, and a set of screens shown together keeps one size.

## Charts you can read, pages that look like pages (live)
- Every bar in a chart has its label under it (Q1, Q2...), bars have gaps, and a chart's title, ticks and legend are set at the stage's own text size.
- A real web page you ask for keeps its own type and layout, like a real site; little apps keep the stage-size text.

## Readable apps, laid-out answers, skills (live)
- Live apps set their text and buttons at the stage's own size, never shrink to half for nothing, and their frame grows to fit what they hold, so no button is cut off.
- Figure labels and step captions are set from the stage's text size; a label sits on its own part's row; a flowchart's lines never run through another box's words.
- A learner's problem is laid out with blanks to work out and one place to answer, with a hint behind a tap; explanations are laid out whole, not paged by arrows; a new problem starts fresh.
- A game you ask for is something you can play on the first answer; "from now on, no code" is kept.
- Skills: [+] Skills lists your Claude skills; pick one (or type /skill name) and the next answer uses it, drawn on the stage.

## Taps that always answer (live)
- A tab or a step shows its own words and highlights, at rest and in every picture: the formula, the caption and the marked rows always belong to the option you picked.
- A recipe, worksheet or quiz you ask for by tapping a choice always opens, even if it was closed, asked for again, or written in the wrong place; a tap that changes nothing says so.

## Apps get their room; lessons hold steady (live)
- A live app keeps the width it was made for (up to most of a wide screen), with the words beside it or below, and its frame is never taller than it needs: a sharing game shows every plate.
- A long flowchart on a phone folds into two columns so every box is on screen, and choices at the foot are set a little smaller rather than dropped.
- A recipe or plan opens on its own parts, never on an earlier answer's words.
- In a lesson, an update never resets the question you are on, your try or your ink. On a phone the question, the paper and Check fit on the first screen, with a short "Not yet" right by Check.

## Everything where it belongs, nothing lost (live)
- Sliders and other controls stand in a column of their own beside a chart or a table, measured at the room they really have: all six sliders of a budget show at once on a computer, a tablet or a phone, and none sits on the drawing. On a very short screen they scroll inside their own window.
- Nothing an answer says is silently lost. When the words do not fit, they are set a little smaller together; whatever still does not fit waits behind a small "earlier" button at the top of the words, which shows those lines in order and closes again.
- "How does it work, step by step" keeps the picture and gives a "1 of 4" stepper beside it: one sentence a tap, the part it names lit up. A thing with a form you can see (a chord, a layout) is drawn, not put in a table.
- A row of boxes too narrow for its words (twelve months on a phone) wraps into rows, every word in its box. A hand-drawn table's rows open up instead of landing on each other, and narration keeps the order it was told in.
- On a phone, labels stay on the screen and their lines end on their own part. An app or a page keeps the answer's words beside it instead of covering them, and a mock-up's buttons and tab bar stay inside its screen.
- In homework, the pencil stays for the next try after a wrong answer, after Help and after "Show me how", on an iPad, a phone or a computer. A pen stroke that starts on a button draws; it never presses it.

## Step-by-step documents that are not homework (live)
- Ask to be walked through a recipe, a lease or a trip and Conduit writes a document of parts you work through: each step with a "Done" tick to mark it off, a choice where the call is yours (how thick, fine or question it, which day first), and help on a step when you ask. Homework help is the same pieces with a young learner's rules.
- Help for a young learner is a ladder of questions back to her, facts first (what do we know, with the facts marked in the question), then what is being asked, then ways to go at it, and then she has a go. Doing a way together is always an ask she answers, never an answer shown.

## Working through a page, a question at a time (live)
- Photograph a worksheet and Conduit shows it as a map: each section and its questions as cards, in order, each with a small crop from the photo. Everything is prepared and checked in the background before you reach it.
- Tap a question and it fills the screen, in the page's own words, with room underneath to write. You try first, with the pen, as on paper; nothing is shown before you do. Tap Check and Conduit reads your writing and drawing: a right answer gets a short celebration and goes on the card, a wrong one gets a kind word about what it saw and one hint.
- Help comes in small steps, only when you ask. "How could I think about this?" is a ladder of questions back to you, one tap each (what do we know, what is it asking, which way could we think about it), with the facts marked in the question, which stays pinned in the corner. "Show me how" plays one way through as steps you tap, and stops before the answer.
- A shape on a grid can be counted by tapping its squares. If Conduit cannot read a question or a figure well enough, it says "needs a person to check" instead of guessing.
- Stop any time. Reload within five minutes and it reopens where you were, your working still there; later, it offers to carry on or dismiss. Close takes you back to the canvas, and + then New conversation starts fresh.
- Units are set properly (cm², not cm2).

## The brain can see what it draws (live)
- After drawing, Conduit looks at its own picture and fixes what is wrong: a label on the wrong part, a label that spills out of its shape, lines that cross, words sitting on a diagram. It also has stage tools to draw and check on the canvas as it works.

## Labels on the right side (live)
- In a labelled picture (a heart, a machine, a cutaway) each label now stands outside the drawing, on the side nearest its part, in order, with a short line to exactly the part it names. Small screens get smaller type and stacked labels rather than overlap. The exploded view is complete when it comes to rest.

## Answers that do not overlap (live)
- A step-by-step answer reserves room for its longest version, so a changing number or a new step no longer lands on top of the text beside it. Tables and text that change as you move a slider or tap a button stay tidy.

## Live table numbers (live)
- Numbers in a table's cells can be worked out from the controls: move a slider and the table follows. A control may be named after the table that uses it.

## PDF in (live)
- Add a PDF the way you add a photo (+, paste, or drag and drop). Its first ten pages are drawn page by page, and its own text goes along with them, so a quote from it matches the page exactly.
- Up to ten photos or PDF pages in one message.

## Tidying up a broken answer (live)
- When the critic looks at an answer and says it cannot be read (words on words, things off the stage, text too small) and the brain can mend it, Conduit now does. The brain is shown what the critic found and writes a few fix lines; they are tried out of sight and kept only if the stage then measures better. A kept repair is shown as a change in place, with "Tidying up the last answer…" and then "Tidied" in the status line. If the repair is not better, nothing changes.
- One try per answer. Never while you are typing, never while a new answer is being made (a new question cancels it), never on a lesson, an app or a mock-up. `/review repair off` switches it off for you; it is on by default. The critic then looks at the repaired answer once more, so the log has the score before and after.

## Agent auto mode (live)
- `/tools auto` lets the brain's browser work without a prompt for each step: reading, scrolling, clicking, typing ordinary text, opening and closing its own tabs. `/tools ask` brings every prompt back. The server's default is `AGENT_PERMITS=auto|ask` (ask unless said).
- Still asked on stage, every time, even in auto: sending, buying, deleting, posting, signing in, and anything on a sign-in, payment or checkout page. Never allowed in any mode: typing a password or card number, touching a tab the brain did not open, downloads.
- The bar says "agent · auto" beside the model; each call allowed this way shows on the activity strip as "auto" and is logged with `permit: auto`. The kill switch stops it at once.

## The bar is one line (live)
- The bottom of the page is one pill, like a chat app's: a + button, the message box, the microphone, Send (Stop while Conduit answers). The box grows upwards when you type several lines (Shift+Enter makes a new line) and rests at one.
- The + menu holds the rest: Add a photo, Take a photo (on a phone), Draw, Save, Transcript, Model and effort, Copy answer, and About (keys, commands, the charting credit). It opens with Enter or Space and answers the arrow keys and Escape.
- Status is not a row any more: "thinking" and "playing" are the box's own hint; only an error or an export in progress shows as a small line above the bar.
- Sliders with whole-number ends and no step now move by whole numbers: 25%, not 25.06%.
- A mock-up on a narrow phone screen could hang the page when a photo's credit was only a little too long for it; fixed.

## Voice notes in
- Tap the microphone (or hold it) beside Send and speak, up to two minutes, with a level meter and a timer. The words appear in the message box to check before you send; a setting sends them at once. Works from a phone, an iPad or the desktop.
- It is read on the Mac that runs Conduit, on the device, and never sent to a third party. The audio is deleted once read, and the audit log records that a note was read, never the words. If the Mac cannot read it (permission not given, a language it cannot do on the device) it says so and offers typing.

## Saving films (live)
- Save film now saves the latest answer as you saw it: the prompt it began with and the taps and controls that carried it on, not only the last tap.
- Save whole conversation: one WebM of every turn in order, each after a card with what you asked, with the sound of any tunes. At most ten minutes; a longer conversation keeps its latest turns.
- Save frames (the zip of pictures) is gone; Save picture and Save MIDI stay.

## Mock-ups you can use (live)
- Mock-up buttons tell Conduit what you pressed (and what you typed, never a password); toggles and sliders follow your finger; follow-ups change the mock-up you have; bigger text; page mock-ups fit a phone.

## Working apps and films (live)
- Ask for something that works (a calculator, a playlist, a practice sheet that checks answers) and Conduit builds a real little app on the canvas, locked down with no internet or access to anything else; what you do in it reaches Conduit. Mock-ups stay mock-ups.
- Save film now saves your latest answer with its taps; Save whole conversation makes one video of everything, with sound; Save frames is gone.

## Photos in (live)
- Add photos to a message: the + menu (Add a photo, or Take a photo on a phone), paste, or drag and drop; up to 4, shown before sending. Conduit reads them (a worksheet becomes a table of its questions) and can show them on the canvas. Stored privately, upright, with location and other hidden details stripped.
