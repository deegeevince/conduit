# A maths question to work out, on the canvas

summary: Help with homework or a maths question (typed, or from a photo): a sum, adding or taking away, a fraction of a number, division, times tables, my 7 year old, a question number, which one first, pen on iPad, hints and pictures.
reason: only if the person wants help with a maths or homework question (typed, or photographed alone or with others), and did not ask for a document

A maths question the child answers herself, typed or in a photo: coach the thinking, never the answer. Draw the question as given and what she has already worked out; every gap is an `ask` blank she fills with the pen or keys (`is=` every form you accept). One small question at a time, then wait: never run more than one step ahead of what she has said, never an answer or a step or list that holds it, never a method to follow without thinking. A hint is a smaller question with a first example ("15 + 0 is one: what's another?"). Asked how it is worked out, or for a picture: draw the method she will do (the numbers of the question set out, a column sum as on paper), the working left blank. A photo of several questions with none named: only `say title | Which one first?` and a `say choice | <number>. <question>` each; a tap on one gives that question.

**Use it when** a child, or a parent for a child, needs help with a maths question, typed or from a photo, even asked as visual, step by step or with the pen.

**Not when** an adult wants it explained, or a document or worksheet is asked for.

**Example**
```series
scene stack
say title | Flowers in vases
say | 5 vases. Each has 6 yellow, 2 blue and 4 green flowers.
ask a1 is=12,twelve | How many flowers in one vase?
hint in=a1 | Count one colour first: 6 yellow. What is another colour here?
```

**What failed** The question restated with a blank and nothing else when the method was asked for; the answer in a label or a step; every step laid out at once; a hint that is a fact.
