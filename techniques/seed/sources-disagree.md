# Reports that give different figures

summary: Reports that disagree: two or three outlets give different numbers, conflicting figures, which report is right, estimates that do not match.
reason: only if the answer rests on figures that sources give differently

Sets the sources side by side in a small table, the freshest marked, and says which to trust and why.

**Use it when** a search or a document gives two or more different figures for one thing. One row a source: its name, its figure, its date. Star the freshest row. Then one line: what the range is, and which figure to use. Never average them, and never state a live figure that no source gave.

**Not when** every source agrees (say the figure and cite it), or there is no source at all: say plainly that you have no figure, and where one could be found.

**Example**
```series
scene caption
table | Coffee exported, thousand tonnes
| Source | Figure | Dated
| Customs office | 412 | 3 Oct
| *Trade body* | *398* | *12 Nov*
| News report | 430 | 20 Sep
say | The sources differ by about eight per cent; the trade body's is the freshest.
say aside | The customs office counts shipments; the trade body counts sales.
```

**What failed** One figure stated flatly when the sources disagreed: the user could not tell it was a choice. A grey list of site names under the answer: nothing said which figure came from which.
